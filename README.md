## About Odonata

Odonata e.V. is an interdisciplinary student team dedicated to the design, development and flight testing of experimental aircraft.

Combining expertise in aerodynamics, lightweight structures, electronics and software, we approach aviation as a systems-engineering challenge. Our projects explore the limits of aircraft efficiency through theoretical analysis, practical engineering and iterative development.

Building on our experience in human-powered flight, we are now expanding into solar-powered autonomous aviation.

## Projects

### Solar-Powered Autonomous Aircraft

Our newest project aims to develop a **solar-powered unmanned aircraft capable of crossing the Atlantic Ocean**.

The objective is ambitious: to achieve a transatlantic flight by a student-built solar-powered aircraft, demonstrating the potential of highly efficient, energy-autonomous flight.

The project is currently in the concept phase. Our focus is on establishing the technical architecture, evaluating design trade-offs and developing an aircraft capable of sustained autonomous operation across an ocean.

The central engineering challenge is to balance energy generation, storage, consumption and aircraft performance within a tightly constrained mass budget. This requires the coordinated development of several interconnected systems:

- **Aerodynamics & Structures** — aerodynamic efficiency, lightweight construction and structural integrity under flight loads.
- **Electrical Engineering & Energy Systems** — photovoltaic power generation, maximum power point tracking, battery management and energy budgeting.
- **Flight Control & Avionics** — flight stabilization, navigation, autonomous guidance and reliable onboard systems.
- **Embedded Software & Data Systems** — onboard computing, telemetry, system monitoring and flight-data logging.

A successful transatlantic flight would represent a major milestone in student-developed solar aviation. Beyond the crossing itself, the project seeks to advance the practical understanding of autonomous, solar-powered long-endurance flight.

### Odonata — Human-Powered Aircraft

Odonata is our human-powered aircraft, developed to explore the limits of aerodynamic efficiency and ultra-lightweight construction.

Powered exclusively by the pilot, the aircraft combines a large wingspan with exceptionally low mass and power requirements. Its development provides valuable experience in aircraft design, structural optimization, system integration and flight testing.

| Specification | Value |
|:--|--:|
| Wingspan | 34.5 m |
| Wing area | 29.1 m² |
| Empty mass | < 30 kg |
| Maximum take-off mass | < 110 kg |
| Cruise power | < 200 W |

The engineering principles behind human-powered flight — minimizing drag, reducing structural mass and optimizing every watt of available power — also inform the development of our solar-powered aircraft.

## Engineering & Development

We approach aircraft development as an integrated engineering process. Aerodynamics, structures, propulsion, energy systems and flight control cannot be optimized independently; the performance of the complete aircraft depends on how effectively these disciplines work together.

Our development approach combines:

- Theoretical analysis and engineering calculations
- Simulation and design evaluation
- Prototyping and subsystem testing
- Hardware and software integration
- Flight testing, measurement and iterative refinement

This GitHub organization serves as the technical workspace for our projects. It hosts software, tools and development resources that support the design, integration and operation of our aircraft.

As development progresses, we aim to make relevant engineering work accessible to collaborators and the wider technical community.

## Long-Term Objective

Our immediate priority is to establish a technically sound concept for the solar-powered aircraft and evaluate the feasibility of its mission.

The long-term objective is a **transatlantic record attempt with a student-built, solar-powered unmanned aircraft**. Achieving this will require advances in energy management, autonomous flight, reliability and overall aircraft efficiency.

The project is currently at an early stage, with the design and system architecture still under development.
